# PT Learning Lab — OriGym Facebook Group Findings

> Historical document: the PT Learning Lab direction was superseded by the current Banner Fitness PT workspace.

Status: initial anonymised thematic scan

Date: 5 August 2026
Source context: private OriGym Facebook group viewed through the owner’s existing membership

## 1. Purpose and boundaries

This scan was conducted to identify recurring learner difficulties that should receive strong original coverage in PT Learning Lab.

The group is treated as qualitative curriculum evidence, not as a content source or question bank. This note therefore:

- records themes rather than member names;
- paraphrases rather than reproduces posts or comments;
- excludes identifiable personal circumstances;
- does not retain screenshots or post-level records;
- does not reproduce OriGym quiz, assignment or assessment questions shown in posts;
- does not treat peer answers as authoritative without checking approved course and professional sources.

No likes, comments, messages or other group interactions were made.

## 2. Initial scan method

The initial pass covered:

- approximately 20 recent chronological posts spanning several days;
- group searches for programme cards, Level 3 practical assessment, anatomy exams and nutrition/exam terms;
- visible comments only where they clarified the nature of a learner’s difficulty.

Facebook search does not provide a reliable total or a controlled random sample. Frequency labels below are therefore qualitative:

- **Very prominent** — repeated in the recent feed and search results.
- **Prominent** — repeated across several posts or searches.
- **Present** — clearly relevant but not frequent enough in this pass to rank confidently.

This is sufficient to guide coverage priorities, but not to claim statistical prevalence across the full group history.

## 3. Main findings

### A. Programme-card reasoning — very prominent

Learners repeatedly struggle to turn a client brief into a coherent programme and then express it correctly in the required paperwork.

Recurring difficulties include:

- reconciling a client’s stated preferences with an outcome or SMART goal;
- selecting a cardiovascular training system that fits the goal;
- translating advanced resistance systems into sets, reps, weights and total volume;
- representing bodyweight exercises and loaded exercises consistently;
- understanding additional-exercise fields for supersets, tri-sets and giant sets;
- keeping the programme card and progression table aligned;
- calculating zones and percentages in cardiovascular sections;
- breaking a large, dense form into a manageable sequence;
- spreadsheet or PDF formula and dropdown failures, particularly on some Mac workflows.

**Coverage implication**

Module 7 and assessment preparation should teach programme design as an explainable decision chain:

`client evidence → goal → training choice → dose → monitoring → progression → paperwork representation`

PT Learning Lab should rehearse the reasoning in an original, neutral programme builder before asking the learner to interpret assessment-style paperwork. It must not reproduce OriGym’s protected form.

### B. Advanced training systems — very prominent

Learners often know the names of advanced systems but cannot reliably distinguish, calculate or apply them.

Frequently confused areas include:

- forced repetitions versus negatives;
- drop sets;
- pyramids;
- pre- and post-exhaustion;
- supersets, tri-sets and giant sets;
- German volume training and matrix-style formats;
- how working sets, additional sets, reps and loads appear in progression records;
- how to choose a suitable exercise and starting load during a practical.

**Coverage implication**

Each advanced system needs a consistent four-part treatment:

1. recognise the defining structure;
2. distinguish it from the closest alternative;
3. build a worked set/rep/load example;
4. rehearse explaining and adapting it for a fictional client.

Error-spotting and compare-and-contrast interactions should be prioritised over definition-only recall. The previously recorded source gap around pyramids is now more urgent because pyramids appear in real learner questions.

### C. Practical-assessment expectations — very prominent

Many posts reflect uncertainty about the shape of the practical assessment rather than lack of isolated facts.

Recurring concerns include:

- what must be memorised and what can be chosen;
- preparing at least one exercise for each possible advanced training system;
- selecting or negotiating an appropriate load;
- using RPE during cardiovascular systems;
- adjusting speed, incline or resistance;
- machine choice versus assigned training system;
- warm-up, main session, core work and cooldown sequencing;
- exercise regressions and progressions;
- coping with nerves and remembering verbal explanations.

**Coverage implication**

The planned **Rehearse** mode should include full-session mental models, not only isolated exercise cards. A useful progression would be:

- guided checklist;
- partially prompted scenario;
- shuffled scenario with a randomly assigned training system;
- self-explanation against an approved model;
- assessment-day readiness checklist.

The product should reduce uncertainty without promising what an assessor will select or claiming practical competence.

### D. Visual anatomy and systems questions — prominent

The scan included repeated difficulty with diagram labelling and selecting closely related structures or functions. Examples covered nervous-system anatomy and respiratory muscles.

The common pattern was repeated guessing without an explanation of the spatial or functional clue that separates the options.

**Coverage implication**

The product’s visual-atlas direction is well supported. Diagram activities should:

- teach a recognition strategy before testing labels;
- use nearby distractors tied to known misconceptions;
- explain the visual or functional clue after every answer;
- revisit the same structure in a differently styled original diagram;
- provide structured text alongside the visual;
- avoid reproducing portal diagrams or question arrangements.

This reinforces the prototype decision to make interactions misconception-aware rather than simply marking labels right or wrong.

### E. Exam scope and preparation — prominent

Learners ask what the exams cover, what to revise and how closely practice reflects the assessment. Anxiety is increased when the relationship between modules, topics and assessments is unclear.

**Coverage implication**

Mock and revision screens should make the curriculum-to-assessment relationship explicit:

- which learning outcomes a mock samples;
- which module and topic each original question maps to;
- which gaps are due to coverage versus retrieval;
- what the smallest useful next revision action is;
- when recorded assessment parameters were last verified.

The product must not imply access to official questions or predict an official result.

### F. Health, safety and professional-scope boundaries — present

Questions included uncertainty about fitness-environment health-and-safety documents and about what nutrition qualifications permit someone to call themselves or do professionally.

**Coverage implication**

Modules 4–6 should favour short scope and referral decisions over passive text. Content should preserve the planned distinction between:

- **For your qualification**; and
- **In current professional practice**.

Professional titles, referral boundaries and other changeable guidance need current authoritative sources and review dates rather than peer-group answers.

### G. Platform and document friction — prominent but mostly out of product scope

Some posts concern missing course videos, access sequencing, broken dropdowns, formulas or document compatibility.

**Coverage implication**

PT Learning Lab should not become an unofficial OriGym support centre. It can nevertheless avoid repeating the underlying usability problems by providing:

- one clear place for each lesson’s visual and reference material;
- browser-native interactions rather than fragile spreadsheet dependencies;
- saved progress and resume points;
- visible prerequisites and module mapping;
- accessible fallbacks for interactive elements.

Current OriGym platform troubleshooting should remain outside the curriculum.

## 4. Priority additions to the product plan

The scan does not change the anatomy-and-movement vertical slice. It strengthens later priorities:

| Priority | Curriculum area | Product treatment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Programme-card reasoning | Original guided builder and critique scenarios |
| 2 | Advanced training systems | Compare, calculate, build and rehearse each system |
| 3 | Practical assessment | Whole-session rehearsal with fading prompts |
| 4 | Cardiovascular systems and intensity | Zone/RPE explorers plus practical decisions |
| 5 | Visual anatomy and body systems | Misconception-aware diagram labelling and transfer |
| 6 | Exam readiness | Transparent module/outcome mapping and original mocks |
| 7 | Scope, safety and referral | Qualification/current-practice decision scenarios |

## 5. Recommended content-design responses

When those curriculum areas are planned in detail, include:

- a **programme-card reasoning map** that shows why each field exists;
- an **advanced-system comparator** using the same exercise and client goal across systems;
- a **set/rep/load calculator in teaching mode**, with worked reasoning rather than answer-only output;
- **card-to-progression-table consistency checks** using an original neutral template;
- a **practical run-through mode** with ordered cues, RPE prompts and adaptations;
- **“what is being asked?” question deconstruction** for dense assignment-style wording;
- diagram practice that varies viewpoint and illustration style;
- revision reasons based on the exact confusion, such as “forced reps and negatives were treated as the same structure.”

## 6. Things the scan must not cause

- Copying or lightly rewriting posts, comments, screenshots or peer explanations.
- Importing OriGym questions or diagrams that members posted.
- Treating popularity in the group as proof that an answer is correct.
- Overfitting the whole curriculum to assessment paperwork.
- Turning PT Learning Lab into a course-platform troubleshooting product.
- Making guarantees about assessor choices or assessment outcomes.
- Publishing personal stories or identifiable learner data.

## 7. Recommended future evidence pass

Before detailed planning for Modules 4–8, run a second bounded scan using a documented keyword set and a defined date window. Suggested clusters are:

- programme card and progression table;
- advanced training systems and each named system;
- practical assessment, RPE, cardiovascular systems and core progression/regression;
- anatomy, physiology, nutrition and mock exam;
- consultation, safeguarding, scope and referral.

That pass should record only anonymised theme counts and curriculum mappings. It should again avoid storing post text or member information.
