# PT Learning Lab visual concept boards

These boards are planning artifacts generated with the built-in image-generation tool. The first three use the same sample lesson and information hierarchy so that differences in tone, palette and visual language can be compared fairly. The fourth is a later hybrid exploration that was considered but not selected.

They are not production interface files, final brand assets or scientifically approved anatomy illustrations. Any anatomy or movement artwork used in the eventual product must be redrawn or otherwise produced as an original, editable asset and checked against the approved learning sources.

## Shared prompt

> Create a polished landscape, high-fidelity responsive website concept board for “PT Learning Lab”, an adult Level 3 Personal Trainer learning product. Show one desktop lesson screen, one mobile lesson screen and a compact design-system strip. Use the same practical hierarchy across devices: simple navigation; “Module 1 · Anatomy & Movement”; lesson title “Joint actions in a squat”; an original side-view movement diagram progressing from standing into a squat with hip and knee callouts; a small progress indicator; an interactive flexion/extension question; “Continue learning” and “Today’s revision”. Make it realistic, shippable product UI rather than concept art or a marketing page. Ensure accessible contrast, clear responsive hierarchy and an adult professional tone. Use no photograph, trademarks, watermark, gamification clutter, medical diagnosis or dense dashboard charts.

The requested short interface labels were: “PT Learning Lab”, “Module 1 · Anatomy & Movement”, “Joint actions in a squat”, “Continue learning”, “Today’s revision”, “Flexion”, “Extension” and “Check”.

## Direction prompts

### 1. Modern Anatomy Lab

> Use a warm off-white canvas, deep ink-blue structure and typography, warm coral functional highlights for joints and motion, pale blue-gray panels, fine technical rules, generous whitespace and restrained rounded corners. Combine sophisticated sans-serif body type with a subtle editorial display face for major headings. Use precise vector-like anatomical illustration. The mood is intelligent, calm, welcoming and credible: a modern anatomy studio that feels energetic without becoming childish.

File: `01-modern-anatomy-lab.png`

### 2. Performance Notebook

> Use graphite and charcoal navigation, paper-white content panels, a subtle squared notebook grid, electric cyan as the primary interaction colour and restrained acid-lime secondary highlights. Combine hand-drawn-style coaching arrows with disciplined sans-serif type, sharper card corners and bold rules. The mood is focused, energetic and contemporary: a premium strength-and-conditioning notebook, not grungy and not school-like.

File: `02-performance-notebook.png`

### 3. Human Movement Studio

> Use warm sand and natural cream, deep teal for structure and primary actions, muted clay-red for joint markers and small sage accents. Use softly rounded cards, subtle organic contour shapes, generous but practical spacing and a warm humanist sans-serif. The mood is approachable, grounded, inclusive and reassuring: a professional movement studio, without photography, wellness clichés or spa imagery.

File: `03-human-movement-studio.png`

### 4. Light Performance Movement — unselected exploration

> Create a new unified design system using Human Movement Studio for its warm light canvas, deep teal framework, three-stage movement teaching and spacious clarity, and Performance Notebook for its bold sans-serif typography, compact controls, electric-cyan interaction states and coaching annotations. Keep the interface light-first. Use deep graphite and teal for structure, cyan only for primary interaction, acid lime only for correct answers and revision cues, and warm clay only for warnings or misconceptions. Use moderately rounded cards and subtle notebook rules only inside exercise areas. The result should feel like a modern personal trainer’s illustrated coaching workbook: warm but active, adult, capable and precise.

File: `04-light-performance-movement-hybrid.png`

## Comparison

| Direction | Strongest quality | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| Modern Anatomy Lab | Clearest balance of scientific precision, editorial authority and accessible learning hierarchy | Could feel slightly clinical if the final illustration and writing are not warm enough |
| Performance Notebook | Strongest energy, coaching identity and rapid visual scanning | Dark framing and high-energy accents may become tiring across long theory lessons |
| Human Movement Studio | Warmest, most inclusive and most natural presentation of movement sequences | Could lose some qualification-level authority if rounded forms and muted colours are overused |

## Selected direction

Use the original **Human Movement Studio** concept (`03-human-movement-studio.png`).

The chosen system uses warm natural learning surfaces, deep teal structure, muted clay movement accents, small sage state cues, softly rounded cards and approachable staged illustrations. The light-first hybrid remains in this folder only as a record of the exploration and should not be treated as the selected direction.
